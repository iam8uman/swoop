One gesture. No targets to aim at.

  1. Hold

    Hold your trigger key (Right ⌥ out of the box). A ring appears right under your cursor, so your eyes never leave your work.

  2. Flick

    Nudge the mouse toward where the window belongs. An outline shows exactly where it will land. Click to step through sizes: half, third, two thirds.

  3. Release

    Let go and the window swoops into place. Esc or right-click cancels. Typing with the key held still works exactly as before.

Every layout, one flick or keypress away

Put any of these on any direction of the ring or on any key. Or draw your own custom layouts.

  • Screens. Next, previous, left, right, top or bottom display. Windows keep their layout, and the cursor comes along.
  • Stash. Tuck a window behind the screen edge. Push the cursor against the edge and it slides back out.
  • Resize and move. Larger, smaller, grow or shrink any edge, nudge in any direction.
  • Undo. Step back through a window's last positions, or return it to where it started.

Prefer the keyboard? Hold the trigger and tap.

Press the same key again to cycle. Every key can be rebound in Settings.

Make it yours

The ring follows your macOS accent colour, or any colour you pick. Change its size, thickness and shape, the preview's padding, corners and border, the gap between windows, and the animation speed. Choose any trigger: Fn, either ⌥, ⌃, ⌘ or ⇧, or several together. Skip apps you never want moved. Export your setup and share it.

Scriptable from anywhere

Every action has a swoop:// link. Use it from Terminal, Shortcuts, Raycast, Alfred or BetterTouchTool.

# one action
open -g "swoop://action/right-two-thirds"

# chain a little routine
open -g "swoop://action/left-half" && sleep 0.3 && open -g "swoop://screen/next"

# your own layouts, by name
open -g "swoop://custom/Writing%20Mode"

# list every command
/Applications/Swoop.app/Contents/MacOS/Swoop --list

Install in a minute

  1. Download and drag

    Download Swoop.zip, unzip it, and move Swoop into Applications.

  2. Open it once

    Swoop isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks first. On macOS 15, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On macOS 13 or 14, right-click Swoop and choose Open.

  3. Allow Accessibility

    Turn Swoop on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. That's the only permission it needs.

Questions

Is Swoop really free?

Yes. It's open source under the MIT license. There's no account, no subscription and no telemetry. Swoop never touches the network.

Why does it need Accessibility permission?

macOS only lets apps move and resize other apps' windows through the Accessibility API. Every window manager needs it. Swoop uses it for that and to watch your trigger key, nothing else. The source is on GitHub if you want to check.

Will holding Option break typing special characters?

No. If you press a key that isn't a Swoop shortcut, Swoop steps aside and the key goes through as normal. The ring also waits a moment before appearing. You can switch the trigger to Fn, Right ⌘, Right ⌃ or a combination.

Can I use Caps Lock as the trigger?

Yes. Remap Caps Lock to Control in System Settings → Keyboard → Modifier Keys, then pick Right Control in Swoop. You can also use Hyperkey or Karabiner-Elements.

Does it work with multiple displays?

Yes. Layouts apply to whichever display the window is on, and screen actions move windows between displays while keeping their layout.

macOS asked for Accessibility again after an update.

Builds aren't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS can treat a new version as a new app. Remove Swoop from the Accessibility list with the − button and add it again.
